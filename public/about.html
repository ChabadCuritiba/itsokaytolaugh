“I was on a quest to answer one question: What is true joy?”

It began as a blog with a simple name and a simple premise: it’s okay to laugh. White page, plain type, honest thoughts. But the question underneath it kept getting bigger.

Everyone wants to be happy. We buy books about it, scroll for it, schedule vacations around it. Yet almost no one can say what joy actually is — or why it slips away the moment we grab for it.

What I found

The deeper I looked, the clearer it became that joy isn’t a mood. It’s one of the most profound and elusive mysteries of creation — and Jewish tradition has been taking it seriously for thousands of years.

The Torah describes a garden called Eden — literally, “delight.” It warns that troubles come not only for doing wrong, but for failing to serve with joy. It promises a day when “our mouths will be filled with laughter.” Chassidus teaches that joy breaks through every boundary. These aren’t greeting-card lines. They’re a map.

Joy isn’t a reward for a good life. It’s the practice that builds one.

Why this site exists

This is where I think out loud. The essays go deep into the sources and the questions. The practices are small, slightly silly exercises that turn ideas into habits. And the newsletter is where the quest continues, one letter at a time.

I don’t have it all figured out. That’s the point. Let’s figure it out together.
